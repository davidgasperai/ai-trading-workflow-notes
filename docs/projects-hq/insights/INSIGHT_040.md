Title

Automate Only What You Can Explain

Core Idea

Automation multiplies a process.

It does not improve the logic underneath it.

If a research process is poorly defined, automating it simply produces poorly defined research faster and at greater scale.

Before a workflow is delegated to an AI agent, its inputs, decisions, outputs and failure conditions should be understandable without the agent.

Why It Matters

AI agents make automation increasingly easy.

A Research Agent can generate hypotheses.

A Quant Agent can write and test code.

A Documentation Agent can record results.

A Trading Agent can eventually execute actions.

This creates a temptation to connect these capabilities immediately.

But an automated pipeline can hide methodological weaknesses behind impressive speed and complexity.

If humans cannot explain why an experiment was allowed, what evidence it required, why it passed, and what would cause it to fail, automation makes the system less trustworthy rather than more advanced.

Impact on Nekonečný Mír

* Define the manual research protocol before automating it.
* Research Constitution comes before Research Agent.
* Experiment Registry comes before high-throughput hypothesis generation.
* Validation rules come before automated strategy promotion.
* Risk limits come before automated execution.
* Human-readable decision rules remain available even when agents operate them.
* Every automated step must have explicit inputs and outputs.
* Every automated decision must have a failure state.
* Agents automate defined responsibilities, not ambiguous goals.
* Complexity must earn its place by improving reliability or efficiency.

Projects HQ Principle

Do not automate a process until you can explain how it should work without the automation.
