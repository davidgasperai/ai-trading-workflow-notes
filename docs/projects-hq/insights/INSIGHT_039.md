Title

Risk Controls Must Assume the Model Is Wrong

Core Idea

Risk management should not be designed around the assumption that a strategy will behave as expected.

Its most important purpose begins precisely when that assumption fails.

A robust system therefore asks not only:

“How much can this strategy earn if the model is right?”

but first:

“Can the system survive if the model is wrong?”

Why It Matters

Every trading model is an approximation of reality.

Historical relationships can break.

Correlations can change.

Liquidity can disappear.

Volatility can move outside previously observed ranges.

A strategy with genuine historical edge can therefore still experience outcomes its model considered extremely unlikely.

When this happens, increasing conviction because the model says the market is wrong can transform a model error into a system failure.

Risk controls must remain capable of overriding the strategy that created the risk.

Impact on Nekonečný Mír

* Risk limits must remain independent of strategy conviction.
* No strategy may increase its own risk limits.
* Position sizing must assume forecasts can fail.
* Drawdown limits remain active even when the strategy predicts recovery.
* Unexpected behavior triggers investigation before additional exposure.
* Correlation assumptions must be stress-tested.
* Liquidity and slippage must be modeled under adverse conditions.
* A strategy exceeding predefined failure boundaries moves toward reduced exposure, not automatic averaging down.
* Risk Agent authority must be able to block Trading Agent execution.
* Survival takes priority over proving that a model was eventually correct.

Projects HQ Principle

Risk management exists for the moment our best model stops being right.
