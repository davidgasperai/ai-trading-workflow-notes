Title

Confidence Must Adapt Before the Strategy Does

Core Idea

When the environment changes materially, the first response should not automatically be to modify the strategy.

The first response should be to reconsider how much confidence we are justified in placing in the existing strategy.

A strategy and our confidence in that strategy are two different things.

Why It Matters

Suppose a strategy was validated under:

stable liquidity, normal volatility, familiar macro conditions and historically observed market structure.

Then a major event suddenly changes:

rates expectations, liquidity, volatility, correlations or positioning.

There are two dangerous reactions.

Reaction A:

“The strategy was validated, so nothing has changed.”

This ignores distribution shift.

Reaction B:

“The strategy lost today, so immediately optimize it.”

This risks overfitting to the newest observation.

There is a third response:

Keep the strategy unchanged while temporarily changing the confidence assigned to its applicability.

That preserves research integrity while acknowledging that the world may have changed.

Impact on Nekonečný Mír

A future system should distinguish at least:

Strategy State — what the strategy actually is.

Evidence State — what evidence currently supports it.

Environment State — how closely live conditions match the Validation Envelope.

Confidence State — how strongly we currently trust that historical evidence applies.

Execution State — what exposure that confidence permits.

This gives us a possible future chain:

Environment change detected

↓

Validation Envelope comparison

↓

Confidence downgrade

↓

Exposure reduction / pause

↓

Evidence collection

↓

Revalidation

↓

only then, if justified:

Strategy modification

Why This Protects Research

Without this separation, every unexpected market event can trigger strategy editing.

That creates a dangerous feedback loop:

loss → modify → retest → loss → modify → retest.

Eventually the strategy can become optimized to a sequence of recent surprises.

Instead:

the strategy remains versioned and frozen while uncertainty is investigated.

A modification becomes a new candidate requiring a new experiment.

Example

Before Jackson Hole:

Strategy version = v1.3

Validation status = PASS

Environment match = HIGH

Confidence = NORMAL

After unexpected macro repricing:

Strategy version = v1.3 unchanged

Validation status = PASS unchanged

Environment match = LOW / UNCERTAIN

Confidence = REDUCED

Execution = PAUSE or LOWER EXPOSURE

Only new evidence can restore confidence or justify a new strategy version.

Projects HQ Principle

When the world changes, update confidence before rewriting the strategy.
